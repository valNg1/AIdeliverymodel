# Operating Model — Overview

The operating model describes how an organization is arranged to turn business needs into maintainable digital products — repeatably. It is the connective tissue between the [vision](../00-vision/manifesto.md) and day-to-day [delivery](../03-delivery/).

## What an operating model must answer

1. **What do we deliver?** Products that create business value, funded and managed as products, not projects.
2. **Who is accountable?** A single product owner, supported by a human + AI delivery team, and clear Business / IT / Platform / Run responsibilities.
3. **How do we work?** A repeatable lifecycle, driven by AI-native Discovery, reference-driven delivery, and human validation.
4. **How do we keep it running?** A Product Operations model that plans the Run *before* the Build, not after.
5. **How do we improve?** A learning system that turns every delivery into reusable knowledge and better references.

## Core stances

- **Product over Project.** Funding, ownership, and planning follow durable products. See [portfolio governance](../04-platform-and-governance/portfolio-governance.md).
- **Single Product Ownership.** One accountable owner per product, end to end.
- **Human + AI Delivery Team.** AI works in defined roles; humans validate and decide. See [human-ai-team-model.md](human-ai-team-model.md).
- **Build and Run are one conversation.** Discovery surfaces Run impact so no product is delivered without an operating plan.

## How this differs from the standard model

The standard model optimizes for buying and integrating, then hands each project off and disperses accountability. This model optimizes for an owned, repeatable capability, with the Run planned from the start. The detailed comparison is in [standard-vs-ai-delivery.md](standard-vs-ai-delivery.md).
